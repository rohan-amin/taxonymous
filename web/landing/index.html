Have AI check your tax return, without telling it who you are.

Taxonymous takes your names, Social Security numbers and account numbers out of your tax documents, on your computer. In their place go labels that say who each one is, so AI can still follow the math.

Why labels, not black bars

A tax return depends on whose document is whose.

Limits, caps and credits apply per person. Black out every name and AI can read the numbers but can't add them up correctly. Taxonymous gives each person and account a label that is the same in every document.

Two W-2s, $190,000 in wages tax year 2025

Northwind Logistics, box 3150,000.009,300.00
Lakeside Grocers Co-op, box 340,000.002,480.00

Social Security tax stops at $176,100 of wages per person. If one person earned both, $861.80 was withheld too much and comes back as a credit. If a couple earned one each, nothing comes back.

Blacked out: both W-2s say ████████. AI has to guess, or ask you who earned what.
Taxonymous: both W-2s say <TAXPAYER>. AI can tell the $861.80 is yours to claim, on Schedule 3, line 11.

What the labels say

<TAXPAYER>You. <TAXPAYER_SSN> is your SSN, wherever it appears, even as ***-**-4321. <SPOUSE>Your spouse, if you file jointly. <DEPENDENT_1>Your first dependent, in every document. <TAXPAYER_ROTH_IRA_1>Whose account it is and what kind, so contribution limits can be checked. <JOINT_BROKERAGE_1>An account you own together. <HOME_ADDR>Your home, apart from a <RENTAL_1_ADDR>. <TRUST_1>A family trust that sent you a K-1. Its name often says who you are, so it goes too.

Amounts, box numbers, payers and form names stay as they are. AI gets your financial picture. It just doesn't know it's you.

How it works

From a folder of tax PDFs to a second opinion.

Taxonymous is one step in checking your return. It makes the documents safe to share, and gives you a prompt to share them with.

  1. About 5 minutes, once

    Tell it whose return this is

    Your household, or a trust or estate. A guided form asks for the people and numbers to remove, and saves them in a file you keep, protected with a passphrase.

  2. Drag them in

    Add your documents

    W-2s, 1099s, K-1s, statements, scans and photos, and the draft of your return.

  3. Page by page

    Review what was removed

    Every page is read again after cleaning. You see each one side by side and can remove anything else with a click.

  4. In your AI assistant

    Share the safe copies, with a prompt

    Upload them to the AI assistant you use, with one of the prompts below.

  5. You know who's who

    Read what it found

    When it says <SPOUSE>'s 1099-R is missing, you know exactly which form to look for.

One return at a time

Each return gets its own Taxonymous file, its own safe copies and its own conversation with AI. Your household's documents go together. A trust or estate you manage files its own return, so it gets its own file. Two filers' details never end up in the same conversation, and each return's labels mean one thing.

Personal returns (Form 1040), trusts and estates (Form 1041). Business returns aren't supported yet.

Prompts

What to ask, once your documents are safe.

Start a new conversation for each return. Copy a prompt, attach that return's safe copies, and send. Each prompt explains the labels, so the AI won't ask for your real details.


          

            
            
          

        

      

    

  


  

    

      

        Privacy
        
Your documents never leave your computer.

        
Taxonymous is a web page that does all its work inside your browser. There's no account and no server that sees your files.

      

      

        
You can check it
After a one-time download, Taxonymous works with no internet connection. Turn off Wi-Fi and clean a document: it still works.

        
Your details stay in your file
Your household's details are kept in one file, encrypted with AES-256 and locked with your passphrase, that you save where you like. Close the page and your documents go with it.

        
You can read the code
Every line is public, so anyone can see exactly what it does with your documents.

      Questions
      
Before you start

      

        
Does it catch everything?
It removes the details you list, in every form they appear, and anything shaped like an SSN. Then it reads every page again, including text in pictures, and fails any page where one survives. You still look at every page before sharing, and can remove anything else with a click.

        
Why not black things out myself?
You'd have to find every copy on every page, and a black box in a PDF often leaves the text underneath. You'd also lose who's who, which is what the AI needs to check your return correctly.

        
I'm also a trustee or executor. What do I do?
Make a second Taxonymous file for the trust or estate, and add its documents there. Its K-1 to you goes in your own file too, where the trust's name is replaced by <TRUST_1>. Check each return in its own AI conversation.

        
Does it work for business returns?
Not yet. Partnership and S corporation returns (Forms 1065 and 1120-S) aren't supported. A K-1 you received from a business works fine on your own return.

        
Do I need a particular AI assistant?
No. Any assistant that reads PDFs works. The prompts are plain text.

        
Will it run on an older computer?
Yes. During setup, Taxonymous times two sample pages on your computer, so before it cleans anything it can tell you about how long your documents will take. Text PDFs from your bank or employer are much faster than scans and photos, and a laptop runs faster plugged in.

        
Is it finished?
Taxonymous is in beta. It checks every page it cleans, and you review every page before sharing, but expect rough edges. If something doesn't work, report it on GitHub.
